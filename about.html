About

Michael LaPenna

Michael LaPenna grew up on the North Shore of Massachusetts and attended Colby College in Waterville, Maine. He later earned a master’s degree in American literature from Salem State University.

For 34 years, Michael taught secondary school English at a public school north of Boston, where he also coached basketball and baseball.

Since retiring, Michael has turned his attention to stories for young readers. His LaRosa Sisters series follows Catherine and Susanna Mae as they solve mysteries and wrestle with questions about honesty, friendship, and faith.

Michael and his wife, Patti, live in upstate South Carolina and spend part of each year in western Maine and southeastern Florida. Away from his writing desk, he enjoys visiting his three granddaughters, splitting wood, reading, and keeping up with Boston sports.

In his words

Why I write

My goal is to write children’s stories that are not for children only. I try to create entertaining tales filled with mystery, adventure and humor that also invite the reader to ponder more deeply the mystery and wonder of life.

What unites all these narratives, at their deepest level, is the idea that suffering can become fertile soil for character development and spiritual growth. That out of the ashes of failed hopes and dreams can arise a greater good.

In short, I want to write poignant stories that depict the reality of life’s challenges and moral dilemmas but that ultimately inspire the reader with endings that are uplifting and hopeful.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Where do your story ideas come from?

My story ideas come from many different directions. Sometimes a theme, some truth about life that I feel is worth telling or retelling, is the starting point. Other times my inspiration may be a specific mood or atmosphere. That mood may arise from hearing a beautiful piece of music, reading a provocative passage in a book, watching a particularly poignant scene in a movie, or being moved by the natural world. And this atmosphere then requires a setting to match. Sometimes a singular event or situation, whether I imagine it or read about it, can be the catalyst for an engaging plot. Perhaps an unusual or intriguing character is the beginning of another tale. I have discovered that the seed of a story, like the mustard seed in Christ's parable, may be something quite small and humble.

Are your characters real people?

Yes and no. All the characters are fictional. But the LaRosa family is based on our son, his wife, and their three children, our granddaughters. Catherine and Susanna Mae are modeled on my two oldest granddaughters; however, whenever the girls say or do something of questionable moral integrity, it is the author employing poetic license in order to create conflict or drama in the plots. All the other characters are entirely made up, although many have been inspired by real people.

What about the settings and the stories’ events? Are they real or made up?

Of the three major settings used in the stories thus far, two are actual places. The cabin nestled in the wooded hills of western Maine in The Offering is one such place. Also, Gloucester and Rockport are real, beautiful, and thriving communities on Cape Ann, a rocky peninsula jutting out into Massachusetts Bay. And the strange and mysterious Dogtown does, in fact, lie within these two towns. This setting is an important part of Laura's Locker. And while the town of Reedy River is invented, the name is borrowed from the very real Reedy River which flows through parts of Upstate South Carolina. And the fictional town itself is broadly modeled on a real-life quaint community tucked away in the foothills of the Upstate Blue Ridge Mountains. The major events in the stories did not actually take place, although they have often been inspired by real life incidents.
